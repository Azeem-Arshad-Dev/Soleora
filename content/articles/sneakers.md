The humble sneaker began as simple sports equipment and became one of the most worn shoes in the world. Its story runs through tennis courts, school gyms, music scenes and fashion runways.

## A brief history

The sneaker's roots lie in the 19th century, when vulcanized rubber made it possible to bond a flexible, durable rubber sole to a fabric upper. In Britain, rubber-soled canvas shoes known as plimsolls were popular for seaside leisure and sport by the late 1800s.

In the United States, the name "sneaker" took hold in the late 19th or early 20th century, reportedly because the quiet rubber soles let wearers move without a sound. By the 1910s and 1920s, canvas sneakers were being made in large numbers for basketball, tennis and physical education.

After the Second World War, sneakers moved beyond sport. Young people adopted them as everyday casual wear, and through the following decades they became tied to music, skateboarding and street style. Today they appear everywhere from playgrounds to offices.

## Key features

1. **Vulcanized rubber sole.** In vulcanization, the rubber sole and its trim are heated so they cure and bond firmly to the upper. The result is a flexible, grippy sole with a thin profile and a good feel for the ground. It is a big reason classic sneakers feel the way they do.
2. **Canvas upper.** Woven cotton canvas is light, breathable and easy to produce in many colors. It softens with wear and gives the classic sneaker its relaxed, slightly rumpled character.
3. **Lace-up front.** A simple lace-up lets you adjust the fit across the top of the foot. Metal eyelets often reinforce the lace holes so they do not tear through the canvas.
4. **Rubber toe cap.** Many sneakers have a rubber bumper wrapped over the toe. It protects the canvas from scuffs and adds to the shoe's familiar look.
5. **Foxing tape.** This is the strip of rubber running around the edge where sole meets upper. It strengthens the joint and often carries a contrasting stripe for decoration.

## Materials

Classic sneakers combine cotton canvas uppers with natural or synthetic rubber soles. Insoles are usually thin foam or textile, though some versions add extra cushioning for comfort.

Leather sneakers are an equally popular branch of the family, with smooth or suede uppers and either vulcanized or cemented soles. Many newer styles use recycled polyester, organic cotton or plant-based alternatives to leather.

## When and how to wear it

Sneakers are among the most versatile shoes you can own. Canvas styles work with jeans, chinos, shorts and summer dresses, and they bring a laid-back feel to almost any casual outfit.

Clean white leather sneakers can even pair with relaxed tailoring, such as an unstructured blazer and trousers, in less formal workplaces. Keep them spotless for that look. For sport, though, choose dedicated athletic shoes, since flat vulcanized soles offer little cushioning or support.

## Finding the right fit

Canvas sneakers often run slightly long or narrow, so try on a couple of sizes if you can. Aim for a little space in front of your toes and a heel that stays put when you walk.

Canvas stretches only slightly, so the shoe should feel comfortable from the start. If you plan to wear sneakers without socks, try them on bare feet and consider low no-show socks to prevent rubbing and odor. A cushioned insole can add comfort for long days on your feet.

## Care and maintenance

Brush off loose dirt regularly and spot-clean canvas with mild soap, warm water and a soft brush. A paste of baking soda and water can help lift stains on rubber toe caps and foxing.

Avoid putting sneakers in the dryer, as heat can loosen glue and warp the soles. Let them air dry, stuffed with paper to hold their shape. Remove and wash laces separately, and rotate pairs so they can dry out between wears.
