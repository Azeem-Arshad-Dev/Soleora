Heels can change how an outfit looks and how you carry yourself. They come in a huge range of heights and shapes, from barely there kitten heels to towering stilettos. Knowing the options makes it easier to find a pair you will actually enjoy wearing.

## A brief history

Raised heels have a surprisingly long history. Persian horsemen are thought to have worn heeled footwear centuries ago to help keep their feet in stirrups. The idea later spread to Europe, where heels became a status symbol among the aristocracy by the seventeenth century, worn by men and women alike.

Over time, heels became more strongly associated with women's fashion. Heights and shapes rose and fell with changing trends throughout the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries.

The stiletto arrived in the early 1950s, made possible by new construction methods that placed a slim metal rod inside a narrow heel. Since then, heels have cycled through platforms, blocks, wedges, and minimal sandals, with each decade adding its own twist.

## Key features

1. **Stiletto heel.** A tall, very thin heel, usually supported by an internal metal core. It creates a dramatic, elegant line but concentrates weight on a tiny point, which takes practice to walk in.
2. **Pointed toe.** A tapered toe elongates the foot and leg visually. It looks sleek but can crowd the toes, so the shape of the toe box matters a lot for comfort.
3. **Arched sole.** The sole curves upward from the ball of the foot to the heel. The higher the heel, the steeper this arch, and the more weight shifts forward onto the forefoot.
4. **Heel height.** Kitten heels typically sit around one to two inches, mid heels around two to three inches, and high heels above that. Choosing the right height for the occasion is one of the easiest ways to stay comfortable.
5. **Heel shape.** Beyond stilettos, there are block heels for stability, flared heels for a vintage feel, and wedges that spread weight along the whole sole.

## Materials

Leather is the most common upper material, valued for its shape and ability to soften with wear. Suede adds texture and a softer finish. Patent leather gives a glossy look but has less give.

Satin and other fabrics are popular for evening and bridal styles, though they mark more easily. Heels themselves may be wrapped in leather or made from wood, plastic, or stacked leather. Small replaceable heel tips, usually made of rubber or plastic, protect the heel from wear.

## When and how to wear it

Match the heel to the setting. Stilettos and pointed pumps suit formal events, evening wear, and polished office looks. Block heels and lower heights are better for long days, outdoor events, or anywhere with uneven ground.

Pointed heels lengthen the leg when paired with cropped trousers, pencil skirts, or midi dresses. Nude tones close to your skin color can make legs appear longer still.

If you are new to heels, start with a lower, sturdier style and build up. Practicing at home on different surfaces helps a lot.

## Finding the right fit

Heels need a precise fit because your foot slides forward with every step. Your heel should sit securely without slipping, and your toes should not be painfully squeezed. A shoe that fits well at the heel but pinches the toes will likely only get worse.

Shop later in the day, when feet are slightly swollen. Walk on a hard surface if you can, not just carpet. Cushioned insoles or ball-of-foot pads can make a good fit even more comfortable.

## Care and maintenance

Replace worn heel tips promptly. Walking on an exposed heel can damage the heel itself and make you more likely to slip. A cobbler can usually swap tips quickly.

Store heels upright with tissue or shoe trees inside to keep their shape. Clean leather with a soft cloth and condition it occasionally. Protect satin and suede with a suitable spray, and keep them in dust bags between wears.
