Sandals are among the oldest shoes humans have made, and they remain the default footwear for hot days. Yet the gap between a pair that feels good all afternoon and one that leaves you sore by lunchtime is surprisingly wide. A little know-how helps you choose well.

## A brief history

Some of the oldest surviving shoes in the world are sandals. A cache of woven plant-fiber sandals found in a cave in Oregon has been dated to roughly 9,000 to 10,000 years ago. Ancient Egyptians wore sandals of papyrus and palm leaf, and Greek and Roman sandals ranged from simple soles to elaborate strapped designs.

The toe-post thong sandal also has a long history across Africa, the Middle East, and Asia. The modern rubber flip-flop took shape after the Second World War, influenced by the Japanese zori, a flat sandal with a toe thong. Cheap, colorful, and easy to mass-produce, flip-flops spread quickly in the following decades.

Sport sandals with adjustable straps and cushioned, sculpted footbeds became popular later in the twentieth century, as people wanted sandals they could actually walk and hike in.

## Key features

1. **Adjustable straps.** Buckles, hook-and-loop fasteners, or sliding loops let you tighten a sandal around your foot. A secure fit means your toes do not have to grip to keep the sandal on, which reduces strain over a long day.
2. **Contoured footbed.** A shaped footbed with arch support, a raised toe bar, and a heel cup cradles the foot instead of leaving it flat. This is the single biggest difference between a comfortable walking sandal and a basic beach flip-flop.
3. **Toe post or open front.** Flip-flops hold on with a post between the first and second toes. Other sandals use straps across the forefoot instead, which many people find easier on the toes.
4. **Outsole grip.** The underside matters more than it looks. A textured rubber outsole grips wet decks, tiled floors, and uneven paths far better than smooth foam.
5. **Heel strap.** A strap behind the heel keeps the sandal moving with your foot. It is worth having for longer walks, travel days, and anywhere the ground is rough.

## Materials

Footbeds are commonly made from cork, latex, molded foam, or rubber. Cork and latex footbeds gradually shape themselves to your foot, while foam offers instant cushioning but can compress over time. Leather-lined footbeds feel pleasant against bare skin and absorb sweat.

Straps may be leather, suede, synthetic webbing, or rubber. Leather looks smarter and softens with wear but dislikes soaking. Webbing and rubber are the practical choice for water, beaches, and outdoor adventures.

## When and how to wear it

Basic flip-flops are best for short trips, such as the beach, the pool, the gym shower, or a quick errand. They offer little support, so long walks in them can leave feet and calves aching.

Strapped sandals with contoured footbeds handle sightseeing, city days, and casual summer outings. Slim leather sandals can look polished with linen trousers, summer dresses, or tailored shorts. Sport sandals pair naturally with shorts and outdoor clothing.

Socks with sandals remain a matter of taste. In general, sandals look best with bare feet and neatly kept toenails.

## Finding the right fit

Your foot should sit fully on the footbed, with no toes or heel hanging over the edge. Leave a small margin, around the width of a fingertip, at the front and back. If the sandal has a contoured footbed, the arch support should line up with your own arch.

Straps should hold firmly without digging in or leaving deep marks. Check fit later in the day, since feet tend to swell in heat. If you have foot pain or need extra support, look for sandals with a pronounced arch and a deep heel cup.

## Care and maintenance

Rinse rubber and webbing sandals after contact with sand, salt water, or chlorine, then let them air-dry in the shade. Strong sun and direct heat can make some foams and glues break down faster.

Wipe leather straps and footbeds with a damp cloth, and condition the leather occasionally to keep it supple. Cork footbeds can benefit from a cork sealant if the surface starts to look dry or crumbly.

Replace flip-flops once the sole is visibly thin or flattened. A worn-out footbed offers almost no cushioning, and your feet will feel the difference.
