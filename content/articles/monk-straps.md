Monk straps swap laces for a buckle, and that one change gives them a look all their own. They sit neatly between a classic dress shoe and something a little more expressive. If you want polish with a hint of flair, they are well worth a closer look.

## A brief history

The monk strap's name comes from a story about European monks. The usual account is that monks in the Alpine region wore buckled shoes that were sturdier than sandals for working and traveling. The tale is often repeated, but there is little firm evidence about exactly when or where the design began.

What is better documented is that buckled leather shoes had become part of men's fashion in Europe by the twentieth century. The double monk strap, with two buckles, grew especially popular in the later decades of that century and has since become the most familiar version.

Today, monk straps are worn by people of all genders. They have a reputation as the slightly daring choice among classic dress shoes.

## Key features

1. **Buckled strap.** A leather strap crosses the top of the foot and fastens with a metal buckle on the outer side. Single monks have one strap, double monks have two, and triple monks are rarer still.
2. **Plain vamp.** The front section of the shoe, called the vamp, is usually clean and unbroken. That uncluttered surface lets the strap and buckle stand out as the main detail.
3. **Leather heel.** Many monk straps rest on a stacked leather heel, built from compressed layers of leather. It gives a firm, low, dressy base and matches the leather sole.
4. **Laceless closure.** Without laces, monk straps are quicker to put on and take off. The buckle can often be left fastened, letting you slip in and out with a little effort.
5. **Elongated toe.** Many monk straps have a slightly longer, sleeker toe shape. Some feature a cap toe, while others add subtle broguing for a more casual feel.

## Materials

Smooth calfskin is the classic choice, often in black, dark brown, or rich medium brown. Suede monk straps are popular for warmer months and relaxed outfits, and they pair especially well with lighter trousers.

Buckles are usually brass, nickel, or steel, in finishes ranging from bright silver to antique gold. Match the buckle tone to your belt buckle or watch if you want a cohesive look. Quality pairs use stitched construction, leather linings, and leather soles.

## When and how to wear it

Black or dark brown smooth-leather monk straps work well with business suits, especially in modern offices. Brown suede double monks are a natural partner for chinos, flannel trousers, and sport coats. Some people deliberately wear the top buckle undone for a casual effect, though that is a matter of taste.

Monk straps look sharp with slim, slightly cropped trousers that show off the buckles. They are generally not the traditional choice for black tie, where a patent Oxford is expected. For weddings and dressy events, polished black monks can be a stylish alternative to laces.

## Finding the right fit

Without laces, you have less room to adjust the fit, so getting the right size matters. The strap should close comfortably, ideally around the middle hole, leaving room to tighten or loosen as the leather relaxes.

Your heel should feel secure, with only slight lift as you walk. Leave a little space in front of the toes. If you have a high instep, check that the strap does not press too hard across the top of your foot.

## Care and maintenance

Use cedar shoe trees after every wear to keep the shape and absorb moisture. Brush smooth leather regularly, apply cream polish to nourish it, and add wax polish when you want more shine.

Keep polish away from the buckles, and wipe them with a soft dry cloth to prevent tarnish. Check that the strap leather around the buckle stays supple, since that area flexes often.

For suede, use a suede brush and protective spray. Have worn soles and heels replaced promptly, and your monk straps can last for years.
