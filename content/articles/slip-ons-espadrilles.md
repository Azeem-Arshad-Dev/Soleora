Few shoes say "summer" as clearly as a pair of espadrilles or a simple canvas slip-on. They are light, breathable, and quick to put on, which is exactly what you want when the days are long and the sidewalks are warm. Here is what makes them work and how to get the most from a pair.

## A brief history

The espadrille comes from the Pyrenees, the mountain region shared by Spain and France. Its name traces back to the Catalan word for esparto, a tough grass that was woven into rope soles long before jute became the usual material. Archaeologists have found plant-fiber footwear in southern Spain that is thousands of years old, so the basic idea has deep roots.

For centuries, espadrilles were working shoes for farmers, laborers, and soldiers. By the early twentieth century they had drifted into fashion, worn by artists and holidaymakers on the Mediterranean coast. Raised wedge versions became popular around the 1970s.

The canvas slip-on has a more modern story. Elastic-sided, laceless sneakers became popular with surfers and skateboarders in California around the 1970s, prized for their grip and easy on-and-off. Both styles now share the same relaxed, warm-weather reputation.

## Key features

1. **Jute rope sole.** The signature espadrille sole is made from braided jute rope, coiled tightly and stitched into shape. It is light, flexible, and pleasantly springy underfoot. Most modern versions add a thin rubber layer underneath for grip and durability.
2. **Canvas upper.** A cotton or linen canvas upper keeps the shoe airy and soft. It needs little breaking in and moves easily with the foot, though it offers less structure than leather.
3. **Laceless entry.** Both espadrilles and slip-ons are designed to be stepped into. Elastic gores, a stretchy upper, or a low, open throat do the job that laces would otherwise do.
4. **Flat, low profile.** Classic versions sit close to the ground with little or no heel. That keeps them casual and easy to walk in, though it also means minimal arch support.
5. **Hand-stitched joins.** On traditional espadrilles, the upper is often stitched to the rope sole by hand with heavy thread. Visible stitching around the edge is part of the charm.

## Materials

Jute is the standard sole fiber today, valued because it is strong, renewable, and comfortable. Better soles are braided densely, which helps them keep their shape. A rubber outsole, either a thin layer or a full tread, makes a big difference on wet or smooth surfaces.

Uppers are usually cotton canvas, but linen, suede, and soft leather versions are common too. Linen breathes beautifully, while suede adds a touch of polish. Canvas slip-ons tend to use a vulcanized rubber sole that is bonded to the upper with heat.

## When and how to wear it

These are warm-weather shoes for relaxed settings. Think beach towns, weekend markets, garden lunches, and holidays. Espadrilles pair well with linen trousers, chino shorts, cropped jeans, and summer dresses.

Wedge espadrilles can dress up a sundress or wide-leg trousers for an outdoor wedding or evening event. Canvas slip-ons lean sportier and work best with denim, shorts, and T-shirts. Most people wear both styles without socks, or with no-show liners.

Keep them away from heavy rain and formal offices. Jute does not love water, and neither style is meant to stand in for proper dress shoes.

## Finding the right fit

Espadrilles should feel snug at first, since canvas and jute stretch and mold to the foot with wear. A pair that is roomy on day one may feel loose within a few weeks. Your heel should stay in place without slipping as you walk.

Canvas slip-ons should hold the foot securely without pinching across the top. If you have high arches or need support, consider adding a slim insole. Try shoes on later in the day, when feet are slightly larger from heat and walking.

## Care and maintenance

Keep jute soles dry whenever possible. If they get wet, stuff the shoes with paper and let them air-dry away from direct heat, which can warp the rope. A soft brush removes dust and dried mud from the braid.

Spot-clean canvas with a damp cloth and a little mild soap rather than soaking it. A fabric protector spray can help repel stains. Rotate pairs during the summer so each has time to dry and recover.

Expect a good pair to last a season or two with regular wear. With gentle handling, many will see you through several summers.
