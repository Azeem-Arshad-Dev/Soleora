Football boots, called cleats in North America, are the one piece of kit every player on the pitch depends on. They have shed weight and changed shape over the decades, but their job is the same as ever: grip the ground and help you control the ball.

## A brief history

Early football in 19th-century Britain was played in heavy work boots, sometimes with nails or leather strips hammered into the soles for grip. As the sport's rules were formalized around the 1860s and 1870s, players began to adopt leather boots with studs, often reaching above the ankle with a hard toe cap.

For much of the early 20th century, boots stayed heavy and protective, and they became even heavier when soaked with rain and mud. Around the 1950s, lower-cut, lighter designs gained ground, along with the screw-in stud, which let players adapt to wet or firm pitches.

From the 1990s onward, boots became lighter still, with synthetic uppers, molded soleplates and designs aimed at specific qualities such as speed, control or power. The rise of artificial turf has also led to soleplates made specifically for those surfaces.

## Key features

1. **Studded soleplate.** The rigid base of the boot carries the studs or blades that dig into the ground. Stud shape and layout vary by surface, from long screw-ins for soft, muddy grass to many short molded studs for firm or artificial pitches. A well-designed plate also adds stability when planting and turning.
2. **Low-profile upper.** Modern uppers sit close to the foot and are kept thin, especially across the instep. That gives a more direct feel for the ball when passing, shooting and dribbling, with fewer layers between foot and ball.
3. **Laces for lockdown.** A firm, even lace-up keeps the foot from moving inside the boot during sprints and sharp turns. Some boots offset the laces to the side to create a cleaner striking surface, while others hide them beneath a fold-over tongue or flap.
4. **Heel counter.** A stiff cup at the back holds the heel securely in place. It reduces slipping and helps the foot stay centered over the studs.
5. **Collar options.** Some boots add a knit sock-style collar around the ankle for a snug, connected feel. Others keep a traditional low collar for unrestricted movement.

## Materials

Leather is still prized, especially kangaroo leather and calfskin, because it molds to the foot and gives a soft touch on the ball. It tends to be heavier and can stretch when wet.

Synthetic uppers, including microfiber, thin plastics and knit textiles, are lighter and hold their shape well in the rain. Soleplates are typically made from hard plastics such as TPU or nylon, with some top-level boots using carbon fiber for lightness and stiffness.

## When and how to wear it

Choose your boot by surface first. Firm ground boots suit dry natural grass, soft ground boots with metal studs suit wet and muddy pitches, and artificial ground or turf models suit synthetic surfaces. Using the wrong type can reduce grip and may increase strain on joints.

Wear football boots only for playing. Studs are uncomfortable and damaging on hard floors and pavements, so carry them to the pitch and change there. Pair them with proper football socks and shin guards.

## Finding the right fit

Football boots should fit more snugly than everyday shoes, with very little space at the toes, often just a few millimeters. A close fit gives better control and keeps the foot from moving around.

Try them on with your match socks and shin guards in place. Your heel should not lift when you jog or push off, and the boot should not pinch across the widest part of your foot. Leather will relax slightly with wear, while synthetics change much less, so choose accordingly.

## Care and maintenance

Knock off loose mud after every game and wipe the boots down with a damp cloth. Remove the insoles if possible, stuff the boots with newspaper, and let them dry naturally rather than on a radiator, which can crack leather and weaken glue.

Condition leather boots now and then to keep them supple. Check screw-in studs before each match, replacing any that are worn or loose, and store boots in a breathable bag rather than a sealed one to avoid mildew.
