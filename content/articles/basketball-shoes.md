Few shoes have traveled as far from their original purpose as the basketball shoe. Built to survive sudden stops, jumps and pivots on a hardwood floor, it has also become one of the most recognizable styles on the street.

## A brief history

Basketball was invented in the early 1890s, and the first players simply wore whatever athletic shoes they had. Within a couple of decades, canvas high-tops with rubber soles had become the standard on indoor courts, offering better grip than leather soles on polished wood.

For much of the mid-20th century, that simple canvas shoe dominated the game. Things changed around the late 1960s and 1970s, when leather uppers, padded collars and more supportive designs began to appear as the sport grew faster and more physical.

The 1980s brought the era of the signature shoe, when star players lent their names to designs and basketball footwear became a cultural phenomenon. Today's court shoes use lightweight synthetics, advanced foams and data-driven traction patterns, while classic models remain popular as fashion staples.

## Key features

1. **High collar for ankle support.** Many basketball shoes rise above the ankle bone, with padding that wraps the joint. The collar adds a sense of security during landings and lateral moves, though fit and lacing matter at least as much as height. Mid and low cuts are also common, especially among quicker guards.
2. **Herringbone traction outsole.** The zigzag herringbone pattern is a long-standing favorite because it grips in several directions at once. That helps with hard cuts, quick stops and explosive first steps. Soft, tacky rubber works best on clean indoor floors.
3. **Lacing system.** Basketball shoes often have extra eyelets, straps or internal webbing that pull the upper snugly around the midfoot. A secure lockdown keeps the foot from sliding over the sole when changing direction at speed.
4. **Impact cushioning.** Repeated jumping puts a lot of force through the heel and forefoot. Midsoles typically combine foam with cushioning units placed under high-impact zones, balancing comfort with court feel.
5. **Torsional support.** A stiffer shank or plate in the midfoot limits unwanted twisting. This gives stability when pushing off and helps protect the arch during awkward landings.

## Materials

Classic models used canvas uppers, later giving way to full-grain leather for durability and support. Modern performance shoes lean on synthetic leather, engineered mesh and knit textiles, often with fused overlays to reinforce high-stress areas.

Outsoles are made from rubber, with softer compounds for indoor play and harder, longer-lasting rubber for outdoor courts. Midsoles generally use EVA or other foams, sometimes paired with gas- or gel-filled cushioning elements.

## When and how to wear it

On the court, choose a shoe that matches your style of play. Bigger players who spend time in the paint often appreciate more cushioning and support, while quick guards may prefer a lighter, lower shoe that keeps them close to the floor.

Off the court, retro basketball shoes pair easily with jeans, cargo trousers, shorts and relaxed tailoring. Let the shoe be the focal point and keep the rest of the outfit simple. Many people keep separate pairs for play and for style, since outdoor wear quickly dulls indoor traction.

## Finding the right fit

Try shoes on with the thicker socks you would wear to play, and lace them fully before judging. Your heel should stay put when you rise onto your toes, and there should be a small gap, roughly a finger's width, in front of your longest toe.

Test some side-to-side movement if you can. Your foot should not roll over the edge of the sole or slide inside the upper. If the forefoot feels pinched across the widest part, look for a wide version rather than sizing up in length.

## Care and maintenance

Wipe the outsoles regularly with a damp cloth, as dust on the rubber is one of the main causes of slipping on indoor courts. Keeping a pair just for indoor use preserves grip for much longer.

Clean leather and synthetic uppers with a soft brush and mild soap, and let shoes air dry away from direct heat. Loosen the laces fully when taking them off to avoid crushing the heel counter. For collectible pairs, store them in a cool, dry place out of sunlight to slow yellowing of soles and fading of colors.
