Ankle boots are one of the most useful shoes you can own. They cover more than a shoe but less than a tall boot, which makes them easy to style in almost any season. With so many heel heights and finishes, there is a version for nearly every wardrobe.

## A brief history

Short boots that end around the ankle have been worn for centuries, mainly for practical reasons. In the nineteenth century, women's ankle boots with buttons or laces were common everyday footwear, often worn beneath long skirts.

As hemlines rose in the twentieth century, boots took on a more visible fashion role. The 1960s brought sleek, modern ankle boots in bold styles, and later decades added everything from western-inspired shapes to chunky lug soles.

Today the ankle boot is a year-round staple. Designers continually reinterpret it, but its basic appeal remains the same: coverage, comfort, and versatility.

## Key features

1. **Block heel.** A wide, solid heel offers height with stability. It spreads weight more evenly than a slim heel, making it easier to walk in for long periods.
2. **Side zip.** Many ankle boots use a zipper along the inner side for quick on and off. It allows a close fit around the ankle without fiddly laces.
3. **Ankle-height shaft.** The shaft finishes at or just above the ankle bone. This height works under trousers and with skirts or dresses, giving the boot its versatility.
4. **Toe shape.** Round toes feel casual and comfortable, almond toes look polished, and pointed toes add a sharper edge. The toe shape changes the mood of the whole boot.
5. **Shaft fit.** Some ankle boots hug the ankle closely, while others flare out slightly. A close fit suits slim trousers, while a looser shaft pairs well with dresses and wide-leg pants.

## Materials

Smooth leather is a hard-wearing, classic option that polishes well and handles light rain. Suede gives a softer look, though it needs protection from water and stains.

Fabric, faux leather, and shearling-lined versions add variety for different seasons. Soles range from leather for dressier styles to rubber with tread for winter grip. Lighter, unlined boots suit transitional months, while lined and lug-soled boots are made for colder weather.

## When and how to wear it

Ankle boots work with jeans of almost every cut. With skinny jeans, wear them over or tuck the hem in. With straight or wide legs, let the hem fall over the boot or crop it just above.

They also pair well with skirts and dresses. A block heel ankle boot with a midi dress and tights is an easy autumn outfit. In spring, try suede boots with a light dress and bare legs.

Black and brown leather are the most versatile colors. Tan, gray, and taupe suede add warmth and texture without clashing. If you only own one pair, a mid-height black leather boot with a rubber sole will cover the widest range of outfits and weather.

## Finding the right fit

Your heel should sit securely in the boot, with only a little lift as you walk. There should be space to move your toes, and the shaft should not dig into your ankle bone. If the boot zips, make sure it closes easily without straining the zipper.

Try boots with the socks or tights you plan to wear. Walk around to test heel stability, especially with higher styles. Leather will soften with wear, but suede and fabric shafts tend to stretch less predictably.

## Care and maintenance

Wipe leather boots clean after wearing and condition them regularly. Polish smooth leather to keep it looking fresh. Treat suede with a protective spray and brush it often with a suede brush.

Wipe off road salt promptly in winter, as it can stain and dry out leather. Let damp boots dry naturally at room temperature. Keep zippers clean and run them smoothly, and have heels and soles repaired before they wear down too far.
